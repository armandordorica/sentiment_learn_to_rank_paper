Academic research synopsis: motivation, hypotheses, methods, results, and interpretation

Section 5 · Empirical study

Cross-stock adaptation of financial sentiment classifiers using pooled RavenPack headlines

Research goal

Determine whether jointly fine-tuning a PhraseBank-initialized language model on a broader universe of RavenPack headlines improves temporal and cross-company sentiment generalization without introducing train–validation–test leakage or sequential catastrophic forgetting.

Introduction and motivation

Financial PhraseBank provides a high-quality sentence-level starting point, but its language and label distribution differ from the time-stamped RavenPack news domain used downstream. Fine-tuning on one company may adapt the classifier to RavenPack while over-specializing to company-specific language. This study therefore increases the adaptation universe from AAPL to five stocks and then twenty stocks, pooling all selected companies in a single optimization run.

Research questions and hypotheses

  1. H1 — Adaptation breadth: pooled multi-stock training will yield higher RavenPack temporal-test macro-F1 than AAPL-only adaptation.
  2. H2 — Cross-stock transfer: the twenty-stock checkpoint will outperform the five-stock checkpoint on companies excluded from both training universes.
  3. H3 — Domain adaptation: RavenPack-adapted checkpoints will outperform the out-of-box PhraseBank checkpoint on strict RavenPack OOD baskets.
  4. H4 — Leakage control: no story identifier or normalized headline will cross the temporal train, validation, and test partitions.

Data and methodology

The unit of observation is a financial-news headline. RavenPack event_sentiment_score is mapped into negative, neutral, or positive labels using the project threshold. Duplicate story IDs and normalized headline text are removed across the pooled universe before splitting. Observations through 2011 form the training set, 2012 is validation, and 2013–2014 is test. DistilBERT begins from data/models/phrasebank_distilbert_best/; checkpoints are selected by validation macro-F1. The strict OOD analysis evaluates fixed PhraseBank, AAPL-only, five-stock, and twenty-stock checkpoint hashes on three disjoint five-stock baskets without refitting.

Interpretation

The saved native tests show progressively higher macro-F1 as the pooled universe expands, consistent with broader exposure improving RavenPack-domain classification. H4 is satisfied by the zero-overlap audit. H2 and H3 should be accepted or rejected only from the common OOD benchmark, since native checkpoint test sets are not identical.

Assumptions, threats to validity, and risks

The analysis assumes RavenPack event sentiment is an appropriate target, the score-to-class threshold is substantively meaningful, timestamps represent information availability, and headline-only classification matches the intended use. Principal threats include vendor-label noise, company imbalance, temporal concept drift, syndicated stories not caught by normalization, dependence among headlines, and implicit test-set tuning after repeated inspection. Macro-F1 mitigates class imbalance but does not eliminate selection or coverage bias.

Conclusion and next steps

The completed training runs establish a leakage-audited progression from one to five to twenty stocks. The decisive next step is to run and report the cached four-checkpoint OOD benchmark, add uncertainty estimates through stock- or time-block bootstrap resampling, and test sensitivity to label thresholds and alternative company baskets.

Section 5 research summary: data contract, workflow, assumptions, and risks
Goal

Adapt a PhraseBank DistilBERT sentiment classifier to RavenPack financial headlines, select checkpoints without test-set tuning, and measure temporal and cross-stock generalization.

Primary inputs and schema

data/raw/news/ravenpack/{ticker}_articles_2003_2014.parquet. Fields consumed: ticker: string, headline: string, article_date: timestamp (or fallback article_time: timestamp), event_sentiment_score: float64, and story_id: string (or rp_story_id: string). Additional market and topic columns may be present but are not model features.

Initialization source

data/models/phrasebank_distilbert_best/: model.safetensors, config.json, tokenizer files, and metrics.json. The classifier consumes headline text only; RavenPack metadata supplies labels and split/audit keys.

Outputs and schema

data/models/ravenpack_distilbert_best/ contains weights and tokenizer artifacts plus metrics.json with tickers: list[string], dataset_paths: list[string], hyperparameters, validation/test: {eval_loss, eval_accuracy, eval_f1, ...}, per_ticker_test: map[ticker, metrics], device, timestamps, and W&B identifiers. provenance.json records reproducibility metadata.

What the notebook does

notebooks/finetune_on_ravenpack.ipynb calls the same shared pipeline as this page: discovers local exports, normalizes and deduplicates headlines, converts RavenPack scores to negative/neutral/positive labels, creates temporal splits, tokenizes text, fine-tunes DistilBERT, selects by validation macro-F1, evaluates once on test data, and saves artifacts. The web app adds background execution, pause/resume, caching, and live W&B links.

Core assumptions

RavenPack event sentiment is a valid three-class target; a headline is sufficient input; train ≤2011, validation=2012, and test ≥2013 represent deployment order; global story/headline deduplication prevents leakage; PhraseBank and RavenPack label IDs are aligned.

Risks

Vendor-label noise and threshold choice can bias results; repeated or syndicated stories may evade normalization; company/time coverage is uneven; temporal drift can lower future performance; pooled training can hide weak stocks; hyperparameter iteration against reported test scores would contaminate the holdout; cached benchmarks are valid only for unchanged hashes and source files.
